# Frontier investing

From *The Anthology of Balaji* (Believing, Finding a Frontier, Invest in the future you want to see). Use on **investing**; also **founding** (where to play). Pair with `tech-value` and `truth-types`.

## Definitions

**Frontier:** unoccupied valuable space. Open frontiers feel like abundance (new rules, few capable players). Closed frontiers feel like fighting over a pie (incumbents, credentials, regulation as moat). Technology and law open and close them in cycles.

**Wealth creation:** technological creation of order — bricks vs a house; random bits vs working software. Voluntary trade is positive-sum even if money looks locally zero-sum (both parties wanted the swap). iPhones were not stolen from someone else's pile of phones.

**Win and help win:** beats live-and-let-live and crabs-in-a-bucket. Tech and venture work this way: you get paid for making someone else richer (Thiel/Zuckerberg).

**Unpopular truth:** core of many investment theses. Diligence (does the model work) is easier than thesis (why). Better to bet on physics than markets; better to bet on markets than sentiment.

**Exceptional in one dimension:** a startup cannot be pretty good at everything. At least one axis must be ~10×.

**Found / fund / join:** three ways to participate in the future you want. Passing on a deal because it is "just money" is allowed; another rocket always leaves the station.

**Personal runway:** savings ÷ yearly expenses. Independence is ideological as well as financial.

## Rules

1. Invest in the world you want built, not only the return spreadsheet — but do not skip diligence.
2. Confirming a model works ≠ explaining why it works. Write both.
3. VCs (at their best) have incentive to discover they were *wrong* and to build people up. Use that as a test of your own process: do you study passes that became winners?
4. "Buy low, sell high" requires acting while the crowd disagrees. Hindsight Facebook-'04 is not courage today.
5. Seed-shaped work: identify smart, creative founding CEOs. Day-trading numbers is a different (worse) life.
6. Don't argue. Build an alternative. Intellectual movements without something to build become status competitions.

## Procedure

1. Name the space. Open / closing / closed — with evidence (barriers, players, who writes the rules).
2. Unpopular part of the thesis in one sentence. If there is no unpopular part, it is probably priced.
3. 10× dimension vs theater (`tech-value`).
4. Founder: intelligence is easier to see than diligence. Look for people who finish.
5. Kill criteria and position: pass / small / concentrated.
6. Role: found, fund, or join.

## Example (investing)

Deck: "AI for legal, $50B TAM."

Frontier: legal research is not unoccupied; incumbents + firms + Westlaw. Unpopular truth needed: e.g. "solo lawyers will pay for drafting, not search" with invoices. 10×: hours per motion, not "AI." If the founder cannot diagram dead legal-tech companies (`idea-maze`), pass.

## Limits

Not personalized financial advice. 1% of the work as an investor can return 10–20% of the outcome with 0% control — do not confuse that with having built it. Capital risk is real even when a five-minute check outperforms five months of salary.
