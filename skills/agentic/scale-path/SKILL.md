# Scale path

Source: *The Book of Elon*.

## Definitions

**New-tech entry:** first version has a *new-technology* problem and a *low-volume* problem. Make mistakes small. First job is **make it work**. Then optimize, repeatedly.

**High unit-cost, low unit-volume:** correct first market for new technology. A tiny factory, much of it by hand, cannot make a cheap Civic. Some people will pay sports-car money. Nobody pays $100k for an electric Honda Civic. Cell phones, laptops, gas cars, air travel, TVs: expensive toys → common goods. Multiple versions + volume make it affordable.

**Tesla master plan (pattern, not a car-only trick):**
1. Sports car (expensive, low volume).
2. Use that money for a more affordable car.
3. Then an even more affordable car.
(In parallel: the rest of the system the product needs — for Tesla, generation.)

Drive to mass market as fast as technology matures. All free cash into R&D and the next cheaper product. The Roadster buyer funded the family car.

**The hard thing:** volume production without bankruptcy — not the prototype. Hundreds of car startups made a prototype. One American car company reached volume without bankruptcy in ~100 years before Tesla (Chrysler, 1920s). Do not congratulate a demo as if it were a factory.

**Signal over noise:** spend on R&D, manufacturing, design — things that make the product better. If an effort does not, stop. Word of mouth from a product people love beats ads, PR, endorsements.

**Give people more for less:** each generation should be more capable *and* cheaper (phones, TVs). Precision can be cheap (LEGO press-fit).

## Procedure

1. Is this new tech or a known category? If known, you may need to compete on price from day one — this skill is weaker.
2. Name v1 buyer who will pay a premium for it working at all.
3. Write the 3-step master plan: expensive working thing → money → cheaper volume thing → cheaper still. What "factory" (literally or metaphorically) is required at each step?
4. Separate **works** / **limited production** / **volume without dying**. Which rung are they on? (`ship-stack` rungs are complementary.)
5. Budget: % to product vs theater (ads, extra enclosures, brand).
6. Investing: if the thesis is mass TAM, demand a path through premium volume and a cash-recycling plan. Prototype ≠ TAM.

## Examples

**Load when** the human plans a new-tech line, or a deck puts a prototype and a mass TAM on one slide.

1. Human asks: AI hardware for every home, $20B TAM, $199 in 18 months.  
   Return: v1 is a high-price tool for a painful user, not $199. Master plan: $2k that works → factory → $500 → $199. Mockup without volume path = prototype, not the hard thing.

2. Human asks: our SaaS is $2k/year for one workflow; should v2 be $19 for everyone next quarter?  
   Return: sports-car analog is allowed. Do not dump price before the general version is funded by the narrow one.

3. Human asks: the line is stuck on one supplier.  
   **Wrong skill** for the bottleneck. Load `factory-constraint`. This skill is the price/volume sequence.

**Do not load** to name the current idea→profits rung (`ship-stack`).

## Limits

Software with ~zero marginal copy cost still has a *complexity* and *support* volume problem; the sports-car analog is a narrow, expensive workflow that funds the general one. Do not use "start expensive" as an excuse for a bad product. Conserving your way to a good future often fails — you usually need a better machine, not less of a bad one.
