# Technology theory of value

From *The Anthology of Balaji*. Use on **creating**, **founding**, and **investing**.

## Definitions

**Labor theory of value:** price or worth is the hours that went into the thing. A chair that took five hours of handwork is "more valuable" than an assembly-line chair that took minutes.

**Technology theory of value:** worth is the *impact delivered*. The injection of value is the tool, process, or system that multiplies output or unlocks a new option. Light bulb vs people holding candles. Refrigerator vs daily ice. Software vs a clerk.

**Physical metric:** a quantity in the world (cost per unit, latency, energy, error rate, time saved, life expectancy). Not a slide, brand story, or "AI-powered" label.

**Unseen value (Bastiat):** what *would* exist if a constraint (often regulation or missing tech) were gone. You can see a skyscraper. You cannot see the city that would exist if buildings took two weeks instead of two years. Uber/Airbnb captured equity; time saved for riders and hosts is larger and mostly uncounted.

**Technology (working definition here):** the set of things that do not quite work yet. Once it works and is stamped out, it is no longer "technology" in the wealth-creation sense — it is infrastructure.

## Rules

1. People pay for impact on them, not your cost to provide it.
2. Whatever can be done over the internet will be. Software leverage only increases as robots execute the physical world.
3. Non-software components get commoditized. Differentiate on the technology that moves a metric.
4. Two mental models: pass a law (force someone else) vs write code (you do something). Prefer code when the goal is a marketplace or feedback loop (star ratings vs hotel inspectors).
5. "Impossible because it failed before" is not an argument. Constraints are physical law and current tech, not last decade's attempt (Icarus vs Wrights). Failures get engineered away.

## Procedure

1. Name the claim: what does this product/company/investment *do*?
2. Labor vs technology: if it vanished, would people just work harder, or would a capability disappear?
3. Metric: which physical metric moves? By how much? Measured how?
4. Unseen: what is blocked by old rules or missing tools? Is the "market size" the taxi market or the movement market?
5. Verdict: **true tech** (metric + compounding + new options) / **optimization** (faster/cheaper same system) / **theater** (story, no metric).

## Example (creating / investing)

Claim: "Internal chatbot over existing docs."

Usually **optimization** unless time-to-answer or error rate is measured and the retrieval/evals compound. Do not fund it as a "platform." Kill criteria: metric does not move in 30 days.

Claim: "This is a $X billion TAM because 1% of China."

Refuse. Size from a real comparable (bottom-up invoices) and from the *unseen* job-to-be-done, not a percentage of a continent.

## Limits

A valid short-term optimization can still be worth doing. Call it that. Regulatory risk is real for genomics, drones, drugs — "move fast" is not a philosophy for insulin in 2026 the way it was in 1922.
