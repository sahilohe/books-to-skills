# Hire underpriced

Source: *The Anthology of Balaji*; *The Book of Elon*.

## Definitions

**Price-performance hire:** smart, hungry, not yet famous. Rabois: hire geniuses no one knows yet. Balaji: hungry *and* can teach us something. Credentials are a price. No-credential + proof of work is often the better buy. Instagram, WhatsApp, Minecraft, Bitcoin reached billions with tiny teams — capital efficiency is not maxed.

**Hungry:** usually early career, or someone you can give the biggest opportunity of their life. Recruited from anywhere they already outcode/outwrite the credentialed.

**Can teach us:** they communicate. Remote work is writing. Mystery-novel blogs fail. Put the point in the headline, again in the subtitle, again in the first sentence. That is how memos and Slack should work. Underpriced relative to potential.

**Hire better than you:** if they are worse, you will keep trying to do their job. Each skill is a **vector**. You may be a 6 in their field (you learned the vocabulary) and a 9 in yours. Approach as a peer who did homework, not as a boss who wants a minion.

**Evidence of exceptional ability (Elon):** career story + hardest problems + decisions at forks. The person who solved it knows the details; a passenger does not. Ask until you can tell. Brilliant people who have not finished school — get them before the breakthrough. Money is not the constraint; exceptional engineers are. Special Forces: minimum passing grade is excellent.

**Attitude vs skill:** skills can be taught; attitude needs a brain transplant. Intellect without character was a stated Elon miss — friends/associates leak character. Purpose + hard interesting work attracts the scarce people.

**Feedback over feelings:** physics does not care. Bad news loud and often; good news quiet once. Criticize the action. Camaraderie that blocks challenge is dangerous. A manager who will not fire cannot demand standards.

## Procedure

1. Is this a hire at all? Automate or buy first (`ship-stack`).
2. Proof of work: code, writing, a shipped thing — not a school. Headline-first writing sample for remote.
3. Hunger: is this the biggest shot they have had?
4. Vector: in what are they better than you? What 6 did you earn so you can talk shop?
5. Elon interview: hard problem, details, who actually owned it.
6. Tiny aligned team vs politics of a crowd. Everyone selected for alignment (`alignment`).
7. Fire path: if standards are theater, the hire never happened.

## Examples

**Load when** the human asks who to hire, how to score a candidate, whether to add a VP, or whether to fire.

1. Human asks: we need a VP Eng from FAANG.  
   Return: that is a credential price. Spec: proof of work in public, headline-first writing, biggest canvas of their life. If you cannot talk shop at a 6, you are not ready to hire a 9.

2. Human asks: here is a resume and a GitHub. Score them.  
   Return: exceptional-ability interview questions (hardest problem, details, who owned it). Hungry? Can teach? Attitude vs skill. Pass/hold/hire.

3. Human asks: why do sales and product OKRs fight?  
   **Wrong skill.** Load `alignment`. Hiring will not fix off-diagonal pay.

**Do not load** to decide hire vs buy vs automate (`ship-stack`: hire last).

## Limits

Harvard/Stanford people are not dumb; they are often *priced*. Tiny-team bias fails when the bus number must rise (`alignment`). Do not confuse "underpriced" with "unpaid."
