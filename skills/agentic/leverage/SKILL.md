# Leverage

Source: *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant*.

## Definitions

**Wealth:** assets that earn while you sleep (factory, program, ownership). The thing you want.

**Money:** IOUs on other people's time; how wealth is transferred. Social credits.

**Status:** place in a hierarchy. Zero-sum. Status players often attack wealth-creation games. Ignore them.

**Ethical wealth creation:** possible. If you secretly despise wealth, it eludes you. Society pays you for what it wants and does not yet know how to get, **at scale**.

**Specific knowledge:** knowledge society cannot easily train (and therefore cannot easily replace). Found via genuine curiosity, not hot jobs. Taught by apprenticeship, not school. Often technical or creative. Feels like play to you, work to others. "Escape competition through authenticity" — copying is what creates competition.

**Accountability:** business risk under your own name. Society then offers responsibility, equity, and leverage.

**Equity:** a piece of a business, product, or IP. Renting time does not produce freedom.

**Leverage (four kinds):**

| Kind | Permission | Notes |
|------|------------|--------|
| Labor | Permissioned — people must follow | Oldest, messiest, closest to mutiny |
| Capital | Permissioned — someone must give money | Scales better than people; 20th-century fortunes |
| Code | Permissionless | Robots in the data center while you sleep |
| Media | Permissionless | Books, posts, video, audio; this book is leverage |

**Permissionless:** you do not need anyone's yes to start. That is why new fortunes are code and media.

**Productize yourself:** uniqueness + accountability + specific knowledge, then scale with leverage. Ask: authentic to me? Am I scaling with labor, capital, code, or media?

**Judgment:** long-term consequences, applied. Under leverage, one correct decision beats 10,000 hours of unlevered work. Inputs do not match outputs. 1,000× programmers exist because of leverage, not typing speed.

## Rules

1. Getting rich is a skill: what, who, when — not restaurant-80-hour-weeks. Figure out *what* before grinding.
2. Play long-term games with long-term people. Compound interest applies to capital, reputation, and relationships.
3. Partners: intelligence, energy, **integrity**. No cynics (self-fulfilling).
4. Learn to sell and to build. Both is unstoppable.
5. No skill called "business." Study microeconomics, game theory, psychology, persuasion, math, computers.
6. Set an aspirational hourly rate **for the work being designed** and cut tasks below it. Do not assign the human a lifestyle rate unasked.
7. Category: be the best at the narrow thing this product is. Redefine the category in the spec until that is true.
8. Optimize the design for independence and output-tracking over headline pay in the offer.

## Procedure

Run this on the **product, company, or person the human asked you to design or judge**. Return the mix and the next artifact (spec, offer, pass).

1. Specific knowledge: what proof of work exists (shipped thing, not a job title)?
2. Leverage mix of the thing: code/media vs hours.
3. Accountability: whose name is on the risk (the human's, not yours).
4. Equity vs wage in the design.
5. Productize: one asset that can run without more of the human's hours — specify it.
6. If a company: are workers paid on output?

## Examples

**Load when** the human asks how to scale output without more hours, or whether a role/product is wages vs wealth.

1. Human asks: turn this 40-hour "AI strategy" consultancy into something that scales.  
   Return: one diagnostic as code+media, equity on implementations, stop selling hours as the product. Proof of work = shipped evals, not "I use ChatGPT."

2. Human asks: should we hire 20 SDRs or productize the demo into a self-serve loop?  
   Return: labor leverage vs code. Spec the loop. SDRs are permissioned and fragile.

3. Human asks: 50/50 with an anonymous cofounder — go?  
   **Wrong skill** for the lock-in. Load `judgment` (and accountability here). Verdict is still **no** until a name is on the risk.

**Do not load** to pick a GTM channel (`distribution`) or to write a req (`hire-underpriced`).

## Limits

Assumes a floor of safety. Do not shame survival jobs. Specific knowledge takes years. Labor leverage is not evil; it is expensive and fragile compared to code.
