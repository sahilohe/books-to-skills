# Execute

Source: *The Anthology of Balaji*.

## Definitions

**Execution mindset:** do the next thing on the list at all times. Rewrite the list every day or week from progress. Easy to say. It is saying no to other people, distractions, and fun.

**Maze speed:** each task is a turn. The important ones move you toward the exit or a power-up. Wandering new ideas is not execution.

**Thiel’s one thing:** everyone in the company is responsible for **one thing**. Each person knows theirs; everyone knows everyone else’s.

**Anti-to-do list (Andreessen):** write what you *just did*, then cross it off. Recovers signal when you drifted.

**List, rank, iterate (Ramji):** meta-algorithm for unstructured problems ("increase sales," "raise capital").
1. **List** the objects (doctors, VCs, accounts).
2. **Rank** with a real function (zip + likely to prescribe; has this VC done deals like ours).
3. **Iterate** brutally down the list with a **limit**. "150 of these. Zero hits → new strategy." Without the cap you fake diligence forever.

Doing things fast often means **one at a time**.

**Align metric to vision:** a quantitative milestone must visibly serve why people joined. Genomics example: 1,000 high-quality couple tests — science that paid off *and* $1k × 1,000 = $1M. Overtime to hit that number was not arbitrary hustle.

**Idea vs execution:** "it’s the idea" is how outsiders think. Novices overweight patents and napkin brilliance. If someone can steal it by hearing it ("social network for pets"), you do not have something defensible. Compare to a working stack nobody can copy from a sentence. Making it *look* easy is hard.

## Procedure

Output a ranked list **the human can run**. You are not the sales team unless they asked you to send the mail.

1. Name the current maze turn and this week's exit criterion.
2. One thing per person, public. If two things, they have none. Write the table.
3. Unstructured blob → list, rank, cap, then iterate. Write the cap *before* calling.
4. Rally line: this number → this qualitative reason people joined.
5. Anti-to-do of what actually moved (if they gave you a log).
6. Defensibility: could a smart listener steal this from a sentence?

## Examples

**Load when** the human wants a weekly plan, a ranked outreach list, or owners for one thing each.

1. Human asks: figure out GTM this quarter. No list.  
   Return: 150 named buyers, rank by who already pays for the pain, cap 150, then change pitch/ICP. One person owns "first 20 paid," not "GTM."

2. Human asks: three cofounders, everyone "does a bit of everything," nothing ships.  
   Return: public one-thing table. Anti-to-do of what moved last week. Next maze turn named.

3. Human asks: is GTM even the right company?  
   **Wrong skill.** Load `idea-maze` / `mission-filter`. Then come back with a list.

**Do not load** to pick the acquisition channel (`distribution`) or the stack rung (`ship-stack`).

## Limits

A hard cap that is too small is superstition; a cap that never fires is theater. One-thing does not mean the CEO has no other duties at 3 people — it means no hidden second scoreboard. Pure economic rationality often says quit; that is `mission-filter`, not this skill.
