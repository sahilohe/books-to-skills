# Distribution

Source: *The Anthology of Balaji*.

## Definitions

**Product is merit. Distribution is connections.** Coming out of school you are taught to judge code, UX, elegance — all inside your control. Distribution is how the thing reaches the buyer: shelf at Walmart, rank on Amazon, awareness. A good beverage still needs the front of the store.

**Commodity distribution:** anyone can buy Google/Facebook ads. That is not a strategy; it is a market price for attention.

**Distribution arbitrage:** an inexpensive acquisition channel others have not piled into yet. The art is finding *that* angle in this time and place, then loading it.

**Startup = growth:** if you do not optimize for growth, a competitor who does will outgrow you. Constant monthly growth requires either more salespeople of equal-or-better quality (hard) or **virality** through the existing base (customers bring customers; sales headcount does not have to scale 1:1).

**Small-n stress:** 10 enterprise customers, one cancel = 10% of revenue, payroll risk, harder raise. 1,000 customers, one leave is noise. Much founder stress is sample size, not "grit."

**Paired metric:** when you scale, every growth number needs a quality twin or people cheat. Revenue → also churn, feedback, **profit per customer**. Users → also retention. Especially when many new employees arrive.

## Procedure

1. Honest split: is the bottleneck the product (`ship-stack`, `the-algorithm`) or how people find it?
2. List current channels. Mark which are commodity (paid auction) vs owned (`media-system`) vs unfair (arbitrage).
3. Name one underpriced channel and the test that would kill it.
4. Virality: what does an existing user do that creates the next user without a new AE? If nothing, growth is headcount — say so.
5. Pair metrics: write the growth KPI *and* the quality KPI. Incentives must include both.
6. Sample size: if n is tiny, treat every logo as existential; do not manage as if you were a public dashboard.

## Examples

**Load when** the product works but does not spread, or a deck treats ads as strategy.

1. Human asks: SaaS works; we will do content + Google ads. CAC > LTV.  
   Return: commodity channel. Name one underpriced test (vertical list, workflow that emails the next user). Pair new logos with 90-day retained revenue. n=8 → churn is survival, not marketing.

2. Human asks: should we sponsor a conference or ship a public changelog that other tools embed?  
   Return: changelog/embed = possible virality (users bring users). Conference = commodity unless the room is the exact ICP and underpriced.

3. Human asks: the checkout is still Figma. How do we get customers?  
   **Wrong skill.** No product yet. Load `ship-stack`.

**Do not load** to design owned media / go-direct (`media-system`) until there is something to distribute.

## Limits

Virality is not a moral good; spam is a channel that dies. Distribution without a compelling product at a compelling price is a leaky bucket (`scale-path`). Paying for ads can be correct *after* you know the unit economics — it is still not a moat.
