# Judgment

From *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant* (Building Judgment). Use on **investing**, **founding**, and **creating** whenever leverage makes one decision worth more than months of work. Pair with `leverage`.

## Definitions

**Judgment:** wisdom (knowing long-term consequences) applied to an external problem. The brain is a memory-prediction machine. "X happened, therefore X will happen" is a bad predictor. You want **principles** (mental models), not a bag of quotes. Quotes without experience are posters.

**Inversion:** you cannot reliably say what will work. Eliminate what will not. Success is often not making fatal mistakes.

**Principal vs agent:** Caesar: if you want it done, go; if not, send. The **principal** owns the outcome and cares. The **agent** does it for someone else and optimizes for themselves. Smaller companies feel more principal. Tie compensation to created value to turn agents into principals.

**Falsifiability:** if it does not make a prediction that could be proven wrong, it is not science. Macro without counterfactuals is not a lab.

**If you cannot decide, the answer is no.** Modern option-space is huge. Marriage, city, cofounder, ten-year company — long lock-in. Spreadsheet of pros/cons means you are not certain enough. Yes only when nearly certain.

**Run uphill:** if two options are even, take the one more painful in the short term. The brain discounts future pain. Compound interest wants the short-term pain / long-term gain path.

**The more you know, the less you diversify.**

## Mental models to actually use

Load these as compressions of experience, not slogans:

- **Evolution / complexity:** prediction is weak; operate as if ignorant of the exact future.
- **Microeconomics & game theory:** supply/demand, labor vs capital. Ignore noise; the market decides.
- **Principal-agent:** every org chart.
- **Compound interest:** users at 20%/month, reputation, relationships — not only money.
- **Basic math:** arithmetic, probability, statistics. Calculus as *rates of change*, not integrals on demand.
- **Black swans / tails:** extreme events dominate (Taleb).
- **Inversion** before addition.

Foundations beat trivia: numeracy and clear English beat calculus and jargon.

## Procedure

1. Name the decision and the lock-in (years).
2. If you cannot decide → **no**. Stop the spreadsheet theater.
3. If evenly split → **uphill** (short-term pain).
4. Invert: list ways this dies. Kill those paths.
5. Principal-agent: who is owner vs hired gun? Misaligned?
6. Falsify: what observation would prove the thesis wrong?
7. Under leverage, spend time on direction, not extra hours.

## Example (founding)

Two cofounders, "50/50, we'll figure out CEO later," one wants to stay anonymous.

Accountability is broken (`leverage`). Cannot decide who is CEO → no (do not incorporate yet). Principal-agent: anonymous partner is structurally an agent. Invert: company dies when the first hard firing is needed and nobody owns it.

## Limits

"No" is a default for optional long lock-ins, not for emergency medicine. Mental models without domain data are still posters. You will still be bad at predicting the future — design for ignorance.
